# ECG Devices Research Repository

This repository contains documentation, studies, and technical specifications related to ECG devices and software, organized by content type.

## Directory Structure
- `/manual/` - Official user manuals, operation guides, and technical documents
- `/ux-study/` - Usability studies, UX/UI evaluations, human factors research
- `/human-factors/` - Studies on ergonomics, workload, safety, error reduction
- `/workflow/` - Studies on clinical workflow efficiency, adoption, interoperability
- `/education/` - Studies on usability of cardiology teaching/training software
- `/benchmark/` - Comparative studies across multiple platforms or vendors
- `/research/` - Academic studies and white papers
- `/regulatory/` - FDA/CE/MDR compliance documents, regulatory assessments
- `/specs/` - Device and software specifications, operating modes

## Academic Studies Collected

### UX Studies (/ux-study/)
1. **Lumikari et al. (2020) - "Prolonged ECG with a novel recorder utilizing electrode belt and mobile device in patients with recent embolic stroke of undetermined source: A pilot study"**
   - **Source**: Ann Noninvasive Electrocardiol. 2020
   - **Study Type**: Pilot study with usability evaluation
   - **Clinical Context**: Stroke patients, mobile ECG monitoring
   - **Key Findings**: Evaluated a novel ECG recorder with electrode belt and mobile device, reporting SUS score of 81.4/100 for usability

2. **Ding et al. (2022) - "Usability of a Smartwatch for Atrial Fibrillation Detection in Older Adults After Stroke: Observational Study"**
   - **Source**: J Med Internet Res. 2022
   - **Study Type**: Observational study with SUS evaluation
   - **Clinical Context**: Atrial fibrillation detection, smartwatch technology
   - **Key Findings**: Evaluated Cardea SOLO™ ECG System usability in older adults post-stroke

3. **Yoon et al. (2021) - "Twelve-Lead Electrocardiogram Acquisition With a Patchy-Type Wireless Device in Ambulance Transport: Simulation-Based Randomized Controlled Trial"**
   - **Source**: JMIR Mhealth Uhealth. 2021
   - **Study Type**: RCT with task completion analysis
   - **Clinical Context**: Emergency medical services, wireless ECG acquisition
   - **Key Findings**: Examined usability of wireless ECG devices in ambulance settings with quantitative task completion metrics

4. **Wong et al. (2021) - "User Perceptions and Experiences of a Handheld 12-Lead Electrocardiographic Device in a Clinical Setting: Usability Evaluation"**
   - **Source**: JMIR Cardio. 2021
   - **Study Type**: Usability evaluation with quantitative methods
   - **Clinical Context**: Clinical setting, handheld ECG device
   - **Key Findings**: Quantitative evaluation of clinicians' ECG acquisition with handheld device

5. **Salma & Arbab (2018) - "Improved User Experience Design on Visualization of Cardiac Patient Monitoring System"**
   - **Source**: ResearchGate
   - **Study Type**: UX design improvement study
   - **Clinical Context**: Cardiac patient monitoring visualization
   - **Key Findings**: Enhanced user interaction design for cardiac monitoring systems

6. **Jakobsen (2021) - "Designing Web-Mobile Based Solutions for Monitoring Heart Signals"**
   - **Source**: University of Bergen
   - **Study Type**: Thesis with SUS evaluation
   - **Clinical Context**: Heart signal monitoring, web-mobile solutions
   - **Key Findings**: Evaluated system using System Usability Scale (SUS) and compared with commercial solutions

7. **Lima et al. (2019) - "Development and Validation of a Mobile Application for the Teaching of Electrocardiogram"**
   - **Source**: Rev Bras Enferm
   - **Study Type**: Mobile app development with SUS evaluation
   - **Clinical Context**: ECG education, mobile application
   - **Key Findings**: Mobile teaching application for ECG with SUS score of 85.3 (excellent acceptance)

8. **Blasco-Peris et al. (2025) - "Exergaming System for Exercise-Based Cardiac Rehabilitation in Patients with Heart Failure: Development and Usability Assessment Study"**
   - **Source**: JMIR Serious Games
   - **Study Type**: Usability assessment with SUS
   - **Clinical Context**: Cardiac rehabilitation, exergaming
   - **Key Findings**: System Usability Scale (SUS) scores correlated with satisfaction in cardiac rehab exergaming system

### Human Factors Studies (/human-factors/)
1. **Zenati et al. (2020) - "Cognitive Engineering to Improve Patient Safety and Outcomes in Cardiac Surgery"**
   - **Source**: Front Med (Lausanne). 2020
   - **Study Type**: Cognitive engineering application
   - **Clinical Context**: Cardiac surgery, patient safety
   - **Key Findings**: Applied cognitive task analysis to improve team performance with medical devices

2. **Mark et al. (2024) - "Mental Workload Assessment by Monitoring Brain, Heart, and Eye with Six Biomedical Modalities During Six Cognitive Tasks"**
   - **Source**: Front Hum Neurosci. 2024
   - **Study Type**: Mental workload assessment
   - **Clinical Context**: Cognitive workload measurement
   - **Key Findings**: Heart rate variability was the most consistent cognitive workload indicator across tasks

3. **Kuo et al. (2013) - "Usability of Portable Patient Monitor in Emergency Medical Services"**
   - **Source**: Studies in Health Technology and Informatics
   - **Study Type**: Usability evaluation in emergency settings
   - **Clinical Context**: Emergency medical services, patient monitoring
   - **Key Findings**: Evaluated use errors caused by interface design in emergency medical settings

4. **Khalatabad (2024) - "Developing Deep Learning Models for Classifying Cognitive Load States by ECG and Pupil Dilation Data Integration to Study Human Behavior"**
   - **Source**: Front Hum Neurosci. 2024
   - **Study Type**: Deep learning model development
   - **Clinical Context**: Cognitive load classification, human behavior
   - **Key Findings**: Explored using ECG signals to study cognitive load with quantified frequency bands

5. **Yang et al. (2023) - "The Impacts of Temporal Variation and Individual Differences in Driver Cognitive Workload on ECG-Based Detection"**
   - **Source**: Front Hum Neurosci. 2023
   - **Study Type**: Empirical evaluation of ECG-based detection
   - **Clinical Context**: Driver cognitive workload, ECG detection
   - **Key Findings**: Evaluated how ECG-based detection of cognitive workload is affected by temporal variations
